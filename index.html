About

Archaeology reveals how societies began, changed and endured.

Yet the field faces persistent challenges in funding research and building wider public awareness.

Conceived by its founder, Omid Majidpour, at the University of Cambridge, The Aevora Initiative is a platform created to broaden support for archaeology. It connects ambitious research with new sources of funding while building wider engagement with the field and its discoveries.

The name

Aevora draws inspiration from the Latin aevum—eternity and the enduring passage of time—and aura—a presence that can be felt. Together, they express the enduring presence of our deep history.

Archaeology brings that presence into view.

Mission

To unlock greater support for archaeological research and discovery.

The Aevora Initiative aims to bring new funding and wider attention to archaeological research.

Projects

Backing research with the potential to change what we know.

Aevora identifies ambitious, academically rigorous archaeological projects where additional support can unlock a meaningful next stage of discovery.

We look for work with clear research questions, strong institutional leadership and the potential to generate knowledge of lasting importance. Our aim is to help provide the patient funding, partnerships and public visibility that allow exceptional projects to progress beyond conventional constraints.

What we aim to unlock

New fieldwork, specialist analysis, emerging technologies, the publication of discoveries and wider engagement with the histories they reveal.

First project

Ancient Sumerians

Lagash, Southern Iraq

The first project Aevora is developing support around is the Lagash Archaeological Project, which is uncovering one of the great urban centres of early Mesopotamia and illuminating the lives of the people who shaped one of the world's earliest cities.

Research partnership

Led by the University of Cambridge and the University of Pennsylvania.
