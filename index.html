About

Archaeological discoveries reshape what we know.

Yet the critical scholarship and fieldwork behind them remain significantly underfunded and too often overlooked.

Conceived by its founder, Omid Majidpour, at the University of Cambridge, The Aevora Initiative is a platform created to broaden support for archaeology. It connects high-impact scholarship and fieldwork with new sources of funding while bringing greater attention to the discoveries they make possible.

The name

Aevora draws inspiration from the Latin aevum—eternity and the enduring passage of time—and aura—a presence that can be felt. Together, they express the enduring presence of our deep history.

Archaeology brings that presence into view.

Projects

Supporting research. Unlocking discovery.

Aevora identifies high-impact, academically rigorous archaeological projects where additional support can unlock a meaningful next stage of discovery.

Our aim is to help provide the patient funding, partnerships and public visibility that allow exceptional projects to progress beyond conventional constraints.

What we aim to unlock

New fieldwork, specialist analysis, emerging technologies, the publication of discoveries and wider engagement with the histories they reveal.

First project

Ancient Sumerians

Lagash, Southern Mesopotamia (3rd millennium BC)

The first project Aevora is developing support around is the Lagash Archaeological Project, which investigates Lagash, one of the earliest and most important city-states of ancient Sumer and one of the world’s first urban civilisations.

Research partnership

Led by the University of Cambridge and the University of Pennsylvania.
