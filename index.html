About

Archaeology reveals how societies began, changed and endured.

The Aevora Initiative exists to widen the sources of support available to archaeology. It develops new ways to fund ambitious research, build wider engagement with the field and create long term support for discovery.

Rooted in Cambridge

The initiative grew out of the University of Cambridge and a conviction that important archaeological research needs new sources of long term support.

The name

Aevum · Eternity
Aura · Presence

Aevora draws inspiration from the Latin aevum, evoking eternity and the enduring passage of time, and aura, a presence that can be felt.

Together, they express the enduring presence of our deep history. The past is not simply behind us. It remains present in landscapes, objects and ideas that have endured across millennia.

Archaeology brings that presence into view.

The approach
01

Research

Support ambitious university led archaeological projects capable of materially advancing knowledge.

02

Philanthropy

Build long term support around research with clear purpose and lasting intellectual value.

03

Partnerships

Develop thoughtful collaborations with museums, cultural institutions, brands and private capital.

04

Awareness

Bring archaeological discoveries to broader audiences through events, storytelling and cultural engagement.

Founded by

Omid Majidpour

Omid trained as an archaeologist at the University of Cambridge, where his research focused on the ancient Near East, following a 15 year career across investment management, private equity and financial markets.

The Aevora Initiative brings these experiences together to develop new sources of funding, visibility and long term support for archaeological research.

Research Project 01

Ancient Sumerians

Lagash, Southern Iraq

The Lagash Archaeological Project is uncovering one of the great urban centres of early Mesopotamia and illuminating the lives of the people who shaped one of the world's earliest cities.

Research led by the University of Cambridge and the University of Pennsylvania.
